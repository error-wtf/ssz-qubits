[044] Our honest answer is: No, not at laboratory scales with superconducting qubits. However, this negative result is scientifically valuable and is in fact consistent with SSZ predictions in the current regime.

[118] 6. Statistical Falsification Framework
[119] 6.1 Model Comparison
[123] 6.2 Falsification Criteria
[124] SSZ falsified if:  across multiple ω and t scalings, measured slope inconsistent with a_SSZ at >3 sigma AND significantly non-zero
[125] SSZ supported if: null result consistent with a_SSZ ~ 0 (this IS the prediction at mm-scale)
[126] 6.3 Upper Bound Example
[127] With Δh_max = 3.5 mm (10 deg tilt), N = 10⁹ shots: The experiment constrains anomalous couplings to <K*alpha_SSZ (95% CL), where K derives from measured slope uncertainty.
[130] Upper bound: |a_anom| < 9 x 10⁻³ rad/m (95% CL)
[131] SSZ predicted: a_SSZ ~ 6.7 x 10⁻¹³ rad/m
[132] This constrains anomalous couplings to < 10¹⁰ x a_SSZ
